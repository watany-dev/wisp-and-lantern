# Learning design

## Audience

Children aged 5–8 (primary) and their parents (secondary). Testers: the maintainer's daughter (6) and 2–3 friends' children.

## Learning goals

| # | Child's words | Concept | In a real AI agent |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Tell it clearly | Clear instructions (requirements) | A vague request gets a vague result. |
| L2 | Hand over the tools | Tools and permissions | An agent can only act within the tools and permissions it is given; too much power causes accidents. |
| L3 | Check it | Mistakes and hallucinations (verification) | Agents make mistakes; check before you accept. |
| L4 | Keep secrets | Privacy | Never hand over keys, addresses or passwords. |

## What we do not teach

- "AI knows everything", "it always does it right if you ask", "AI is your friend or family".
- Promotion of any specific product. Fear as a way to make children obey.

## Trials

| # | Trial | Teaches | How you win | Job of the spookiness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tell it clearly (The Dark Lane) | L1 | Pick picture cards (color, object; place is not built); the helper brings what the cards say, and for a card left out it chooses itself, so a vague request brings a different item. Both cards find the wish. | Darkness = what the helper does not know. The game is a 60-second score attack: only telling the helper both the color and the object finds the wish, so the score rewards the lesson (`04-score-attack`). |
| 2 | Hand over the tools (The Locked Shed) | L2 | Five puzzles (a lock, leaves, boards, a rope, a cat on the roof), five tools (key, broom, hammer, scissors, ladder): hand over one to free the cat, as many cats as possible in 60 seconds. The wisp tries exactly the tool it is given; each puzzle has one tool that fits, and the child can try again without limit (`05-trial-grant-tools`, revision 2). | Locks and doors = permission. The fixed rules are a staging device for one idea (what the helper was given decides what it can do, and the right tool depends on the problem), not a claim about real AI; the key belongs to the game's shed. |
| 3 | Check it (The Wisp Field) | L3 | The wisp brings 5 stars and 1–2 are fakes. A fake shines brighter than a real star (a larger, whiter halo) and its rim is a ring of dots where a real star's is one line, so it can be told by looking, with no color and no sound, from the first screen. Tap a star to look at it closely, then decide "real" or "fake". A right decision sorts the star; a wrong one sorts nothing, says what happened kindly, and the child looks again, with no limit and no cost, so the game never decides for the child. When all five are sorted, keep only the real ones. Two rounds (one fake, then two) clear the trial, which ends on a result screen with a way back to the title. The wisp mixed a fake up by mistake, not on purpose; it is never a liar or an enemy. No clock or score (`06-trial-verify-stars`). | Fakes = marsh-light look-alikes that shine too brightly. The child-facing word is "fake star", not "wisp", because the helper is the wisp. The visible clue and the one-tap judgement are a staging device for "look closely and decide before you accept", not a claim that checking real AI is that easy. |
| 4 | Keep secrets (The Listening Shadows) | L4 | Hand over only the items that are safe to give (house key, name, candy). | Shadows = third parties who listen in. |
