# Learning design

## Audience

Children aged 5–8 (primary) and their parents (secondary). Testers: the maintainer's daughter (6) and 2–3 friends' children.

## Learning goals

| # | Child's words | Concept | In a real AI agent |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Tell it clearly | Clear instructions (requirements) | A vague request gets a vague result. |
| L2 | Hand over the tools | Tools and permissions | An agent can only act within the tools and permissions it is given; too much power causes accidents. |
| L3 | Check it | Mistakes and hallucinations (verification) | Agents make mistakes; check before you accept. |
| L4 | Keep secrets | Privacy | Never hand over keys, addresses or passwords. |

## What we do not teach

- "AI knows everything", "it always does it right if you ask", "AI is your friend or family".
- Promotion of any specific product. Fear as a way to make children obey.

## Trials

| # | Trial | Teaches | How you win | Job of the spookiness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tell it clearly (The Dark Lane) | L1 | Pick 2–3 picture cards (color, object, place); the helper searches exactly by the cards. | Darkness = what the helper does not know. |
| 2 | Hand over the tools (The Locked Shed) | L2 | Give one tool (key, broom or hammer) to let a cat out of a locked shed. | Locks and doors = permission. |
| 3 | Check it (The Wisp Field) | L3 | 1–2 of the 5 stars the helper brings are wisps; tap to check before accepting. | Wisps = fakes that shine brighter than the real thing. |
| 4 | Keep secrets (The Listening Shadows) | L4 | Hand over only the items that are safe to give (house key, name, candy). | Shadows = third parties who listen in. |
