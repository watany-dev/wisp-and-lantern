# Learning design

## Audience

Children aged 5–8 (primary) and their parents (secondary). Testers: the maintainer's daughter (6) and 2–3 friends' children.

## Learning goals

| # | Child's words | Concept | In a real AI agent |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Tell it clearly | Clear instructions (requirements) | A vague request gets a vague result. |
| L2 | Hand over the tools | Tools and permissions | An agent can only act within the tools and permissions it is given; too much power causes accidents. |
| L3 | Check it | Mistakes and hallucinations (verification) | Agents make mistakes; check before you accept. |
| L4 | Keep secrets | Privacy | Never hand over keys, addresses or passwords. |
| L5 | Tell it what's wrong | Feedback: say where and how | When a result is almost right, say which part to change and how, instead of asking again from the start. |

## What we do not teach

- "AI knows everything", "it always does it right if you ask", "AI is your friend or family".
- Promotion of any specific product. Fear as a way to make children obey.

## Trials

| # | Trial | Teaches | How you win | Job of the spookiness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tell it clearly (The Dark Lane) | L1 | Pick picture cards (color, object; place is not built); the helper brings what the cards say, and for a card left out it chooses itself, so a vague request brings a different item. Both cards find the wish. | Darkness = what the helper does not know. The game is a 60-second score attack: only telling the helper both the color and the object finds the wish, so the score rewards the lesson (`04-score-attack`). |
| 2 | Hand over the tools (The Locked Shed) | L2 | Five puzzles (a lock, leaves, boards, a rope, a cat on the roof), five tools (key, broom, hammer, scissors, ladder): hand over one to free the cat, as many cats as possible in 60 seconds. The wisp tries exactly the tool it is given; each puzzle has one tool that fits, and the child can try again without limit (`05-trial-grant-tools`, revision 2). | Locks and doors = permission. The fixed rules are a staging device for one idea (what the helper was given decides what it can do, and the right tool depends on the problem), not a claim about real AI; the key belongs to the game's shed. |
| 3 | Check it (The Wisp Field) | L3 | The wisp brings 5 stars and 1–2 are fakes. A fake shines brighter than a real star (a larger, whiter halo) and its rim is a ring of dots where a real star's is one line, so it can be told by looking, with no color and no sound, from the first screen. Tap a star to look at it closely, then decide "real" or "fake". A right decision sorts the star and scores (100, plus 50 for each right decision in a row, up to 4); a wrong one sorts nothing, scores nothing and ends the streak, says what happened kindly, and the child looks again, with no limit and no time lost, so the game never decides for the child. When all five are sorted, keep only the real ones, and the next five come. It is a 60-second time attack with trial 1's clock, score and end screen, and a Title button in every stage; a child who guesses loses the streak, one who looks scores more. The wisp mixed a fake up by mistake, not on purpose; it is never a liar or an enemy (`06-trial-verify-stars`, revision 3). | Fakes = marsh-light look-alikes that shine too brightly. The child-facing word is "fake star", not "wisp", because the helper is the wisp. The visible clue, the one-tap judgement and the quick clock are a staging device for "look closely and decide before you accept", not a claim that checking real AI is that easy or that fast. |
| 4 | Keep secrets (The Listening Shadows) | L4 | One thing at a time is in front of the wisp, and a shadow listens beside it. Decide: "give" or "secret". A house key, an address and a password are secrets and are given to nobody; a candy, a ball and a flower are fine to give. A right decision scores (100, plus 50 for each right decision in a row, up to 4); a wrong one scores nothing and ends the streak. A given item is "heard" by the shadow (it appears in the shadow's bubble), a kept one is not (quiet dots, a padlock on the tile), and a closing line says what kind of thing it was. A secret that was given cannot be taken back in that round and costs only its points, with no time lost. It is a 60-second time attack with trial 1's clock, score and end screen and a Title button in every stage (`07-trial-keep-secrets`). | Shadows = third parties who may be listening, never enemies: the words say what the shadow heard, never that something bad will happen. The six items, the fixed kinds and the one-tap decision are a staging device for "give a helper only what is fine for others to hear", not a claim about how a real AI handles what it is given. A first name is not used: whether it is a secret depends on who asks, which a 6-year-old cannot judge, so the table's earlier "name" is replaced by the address. |
| 5 | Tell it what's wrong (The Flickering Lantern) | L5 | Each round has a wish: a lantern of three parts (a handle: ring, bow or bell; a roof: pointy, round or flat; a light: candle, star or moon). The wisp brings one that matches it in every part but one, and that part flickers and has a frame of dots. Tap it (where), then pick one of two cards for it (how); the wisp changes exactly that part to exactly that look and nothing else. The wish's look lights the lantern and scores (100, plus 50 for each right card in a row, up to 4); another look changes the part, which still flickers, and ends the streak, with no limit and no time lost. A tap on a steady part says it is fine, at no cost. There is no way to ask for a whole new lantern. It is a 60-second time attack with trial 1's clock, score and end screen and a Title button in every stage (`08-trial-tell-whats-wrong`). | Flicker = the one part the helper got different; it shows where, never how. The three parts, the nine looks and the two cards are a staging device for "say what to change, not start over", not a claim that a real helper always changes only what it is told. The wisp did what it was told and is never blamed. |
| 6 | Use it all (The Hollow Crown) | L1–L5 | Three lamps, one chain each: tell the wisp the lamp (a roof card and a light card; with one, the wisp asks for the other and waits), give it the tool for the job at the shed's door (leaves, boards or a rope; a secret among the three things is not a tool and moves nothing on), ask it to go (it tries exactly that tool), check the lamp it made against the wish ("same" or "different"; the wisp always slips in one part, and only "same" on the wish is accepted), and fix the part that is not the same (tap it, then one of two cards; only that part changes, and the child checks again). A skipped step does not work: only the step's own button moves the chain on, and nothing goes back to the tell. There is no way to ask for the whole lamp again. No clock and no score; the third lit lamp clears the trial, and the clear has play again and Title (`09-trial-hollow-crown`). | The dark town is a town at night waiting to be lit, not a danger; the chain relights it lamp by lamp. The five fixed steps, the one slip and the one tool that fits are a staging device for "a job with a helper is these steps in this order", not a claim that a real helper works in exactly these steps, always slips in one part, or keeps secrets for the child. The wisp did what it was told and is never blamed. |
